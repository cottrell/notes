title: "Part I: SIPPs and the IHT deal"

From April 2027, SIPP funds are brought into IHT without grandfathering. Money paid in under terms that were not subject to IHT on death will now be counted in the estate.

The scheme terms were documented. The IHT arrangement (the deal terms) was not fixed by contract. Some providers did warn that tax rules could change. The question is whether that general warning conveyed the specific risk that a future change would apply to money already committed under the old IHT treatment. If it did not — call it the inadequate disclosure scenario — savers were not sufficiently informed about a material risk in what they were buying. That is a mis-selling argument. If savers were instead entitled to treat the IHT terms as part of the deal — call it the fixed deal scenario — then changing them now is a breach of what they were sold.

The warnings are worth dating and crediting:

- **2018:** [AJ Bell Youinvest's tax guide](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/sites/default/files/guide/file/AJBYI_Top_ten_tax_tips_2018.pdf) said tax rules might change in future. The same guide discussed SIPP death benefits.
- **18 May 2021:** [Standard Life's pension and tax guide](https://www.standardlife.co.uk/articles/article-page/2021-tax-year-changes) said laws and tax rules might change in future. This was general pension guidance, not SIPP key features.
- **2 March 2023:** [AJ Bell's SIPP tax article](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/news/three-top-tax-tips-sipps) again warned that pension and tax rules might change.
- **As at 25 March 2024:** [Hargreaves Lansdown's SIPP key features](https://www.hl.co.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/4974210/SIPP-key-features.pdf), marked correct as at that date, listed changing tax benefits and government pension policy among the risks. It also said the government could change the rules. The document does not establish the day this wording first appeared.

These are real warnings, issued before the October 2024 announcement. None of the examples above specifically says that a later IHT change could remove the old treatment from contributions already made. Whether the broad warning was enough is the live question, not whether any warning existed.

Under the fixed deal scenario, the straightforward resolution would have been to close the old deal to new contributions and open a new one under explicit terms. Existing funds continue under the rules they were committed under; new contributions are made under the new terms. Providers run two account types for a time. That is unremarkable. The problem is not that the law changed — it is that the change was applied to capital already committed under a different deal.

"Because it's the government" isn't a satisfying resolution. It's just an answer about who has the power to avoid the question.
