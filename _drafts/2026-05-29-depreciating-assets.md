title: Depreciating Assets
date: 2026-05-29
---

"Tech debt" is the wrong metaphor. Debt implies an obligation you must repay. Most software is more like industrial machinery — a depreciating asset. The problem isn't that you owe someone money. The problem is that the asset is rotting.

But even that undersells the real objective. The goal isn't maximizing the value of today's assets. It's maximizing future reachable states — preserving optionality. A great engineer doesn't primarily produce code. They produce migration paths, escape hatches, future choices. A mediocre engineer might produce 100k lines of code. A great engineer might produce the thing that lets the company survive five years later.

This reframes what deletion means. A prototype discarded after three days may have enormous ROI if it collapses uncertainty about an important decision. The thing being compounded is not code. It is knowledge plus capability. Bad engineers optimize for asset accumulation. Good engineers optimize for option generation.

Nobody admires a construction company because it owns scaffolding. They admire it because it can reliably build the next thing. The codebase is not the product — it is the residue of the process that produced the product. Code is frozen decision-making. Every line says: *at some point we believed this was the correct thing to do.* The real asset is the process that generated those decisions and can generate new ones.

Code is a product-shaped action. The action matters more than the shape it leaves behind.
